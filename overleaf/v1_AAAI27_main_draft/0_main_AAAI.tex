\begin{abstract}

%World models have recently attracted significant attention as a framework for learning latent representations that preserve the structure and dynamics of the physical world. Among recent approaches, Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) are especially compelling because they predict masked or future content directly in embedding space, avoiding costly pixel-level generation while promoting semantic abstraction.

World models aim to capture and predict the structure and dynamics of the physical world, and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) are a compelling direction. We study a largely unexplored regime: populous, crowded, and chaotic Global South urban environments, which we call \textbf{DENSEWORLD}, where soft spatial boundaries, extreme agent heterogeneity, persistent occlusion, and rapid social negotiation replace the lane-structured assumptions behind existing evaluations. We release DENSEWORLD 1.0: 115,687 videos (276 hours) of drive-through, walk-through, and aerial footage, each 4 to 10 seconds, spanning 22 Indian cities and 714 long-form sources, of which 86,831 carry automatically derived layout, agent, and interaction targets. Ten off-the-shelf frozen encoders (DINOv2, I-JEPA, LeJEPA, and V-JEPA~1/2.0/2.1) all collapse into a narrow 37.5\% to 44.4\% band on an 11-way motion-class probe with a 19.5\% majority baseline, whereas DENSEWORLD-adapted encoders reach 50.3\% to 53.2\%.

We introduce \textbf{\emph{FactorJEPA}}, which makes world structure a first-class predictive primitive. Rather than adapting a JEPA predictor on undifferentiated video, it performs \emph{predictor surgery}: a three-stage curriculum over automatically separated layout, agent, and interaction views, updating at most 8/48 (2B) and 6/40 (1B) encoder blocks with the predictor. Trained on 86,831 factor-annotated videos and evaluated on 23,106 held-out samples (1B V-JEPA~2.1), it cuts future-latent L1 by 10.6\%, causal future-block L1 by 8.9\% and mask-ratio slope by 27.4\% against the frozen backbone, and raises motion-cosine separation to 15.3$\times$ the frozen value, each gain exceeding 70$\times$ the paired 95\% confidence interval. On both prediction diagnostics it separates in its favour against all six adaptation techniques we evaluate, namely full fine-tuning, linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT), low-rank adaptation (LoRA), weight-decomposed low-rank adaptation (DoRA), automatic relative-gradient-norm block selection (Auto-RGN), and vanilla continual self-supervised learning (continual-SSL), at both backbone scales, giving 24 of 24 paired wins, and beats the strongest full-scale baseline, LoRA, by +2.8\%, +1.6\%, +25.2\% and +17.8\%.

20-arm (2B) and 14-arm (1B) studies show rankings replicate across backbone scale at $\rho=0.895$ to $0.978$, establishing the 1B model as a half-cost proxy for 2B selection. On a 10k screening subset, factorization concedes maximally linear motion readout to full fine-tuning ($-44.9$\% at 2B, $-33.4$\% at 1B), a trade-off that disappears at full scale, where FactorJEPA leads motion by +25.2\% and gains more than LoRA on all four diagnostics. We release the dataset, checkpoints and the complete 15-diagnostic evaluation harness.





\end{abstract}
