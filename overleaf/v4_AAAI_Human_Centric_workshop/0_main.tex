\title{FactorJEPA on DENSEWORLD-115k: Can a World Model Predict Crowded, Chaotic South Asian Streets?}

\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A mobility agent that drives, walks, or dispatches in a crowded city has to anticipate what pedestrians, two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, carts, and animals will do next, often while they are partly hidden. Video world models such as V-JEPA 2.1 forecast the future in embedding space, but they are trained and evaluated mostly on lower-density, lane-structured traffic. We build DENSEWORLD-115k, 115,687 clips (276 hours) cut from 714 public YouTube videos of 22 Indian cities, released as metadata only, and FactorJEPA, which replaces V-JEPA's monolithic predictor with separate layout, agent, and interaction channels. Compared with seven adaptation methods under identical data and budgets, one fixed FactorJEPA configuration predicts masked and future content better at both 2B and 1B scale, and after full 115k-clip training it separates from the strongest rival on all four diagnostics of predictive structure by 13.9 to 43.3 widths of the paired 95\% confidence interval. Competing methods stay ahead on temporal coherence. We discuss what such a traffic-scene world model can and cannot yet offer mobility agents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Agentic mobility services, from driver assistants to delivery robots and fleet dispatchers, act among road users whose next move they must predict. World models of traffic scenes supply that prediction. Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) such as V-JEPA 2.1 \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21} learn it without labels by predicting masked or future video content as embeddings rather than pixels. Their predictor is monolithic: nothing in it keeps the road layout, the individual agents, and the interactions between them apart inside the predicted future. That matters least on a marked highway and most where these factors are tightly coupled.

South Asian streets are such a regime. Road edges are negotiated rather than painted, pedestrians and vehicles of very different speeds share one right of way, clutter keeps agents partly hidden, and motion follows negotiation more than lane rules. Driving benchmarks such as BDD100K \cite{yu2020bdd100k} and nuScenes \cite{caesar2020nuscenes} sample this regime rarely. We contribute:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DENSEWORLD-115k}, a 276-hour drive-through, walk-through, and aerial video corpus of 22 Indian cities, released as reconstructible metadata.
    \item \textbf{FactorJEPA}, a V-JEPA predictor that composes the future from layout, agent, and interaction channels, with no extra inputs at inference.
    \item A controlled comparison against seven adaptation methods at two model scales and two data scales, reporting one fixed configuration on 13 diagnostics, including those it loses.
\end{itemize}

\section{DENSEWORLD-115k}

The corpus cuts 714 public long-form YouTube videos (480p) from 22 Tier-1 and Tier-2 Indian cities into 115,687 clips of 4 to 10 seconds with scene and shot-boundary detection, 276 hours in total (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}, top). It covers drive-through, walk-through, and aerial viewpoints across markets, residential lanes, transit corridors, junctions, flyovers, heritage sites, and beaches, at different times of day and in different weather. Faces and licence plates are blurred before any processing. For 86,831 clips, an automatic pipeline derives layout, agent, and interaction targets: Grounding DINO \cite{liu2024groundingdino} detects agents on anchor frames, SAM~3 \cite{carion2025sam3} tracks their masks through the clip, and agent pairs are linked by proximity and relative motion.

Against a reference set of BDD100K and nuScenes clips grouped by the same scene types, DENSEWORLD has more agents per clip and a larger share of pixels covered by agents on all six types we compared (Figure~\ref{fig:density}); we measured only these two axes, not occlusion or interaction pressure.

\paragraph{Release.} The videos stay under their creators' copyright and are not redistributed. The dataset card ships the source list, the clip manifest with timestamps, and a deterministic script that rebuilds every clip from YouTube.

\section{FactorJEPA}

FactorJEPA keeps the V-JEPA 2.1 encoder, its momentum target encoder, the masking policy, and the prediction targets, and replaces only the predictor. For each target token it predicts layout, agent, and interaction coordinates $C_L$, $C_A$, $C_I$ (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}, bottom) and composes the future embedding through learned dictionaries,
\begin{equation}
\widehat Y = C_L A_L^{\top} + C_A A_A^{\top} + C_I A_I^{\top}.
\end{equation}
A soft visibility gate re-weights agents by how visible they are instead of discarding occluded ones, and interaction coordinates are sparse weighted sums over candidate agent pairs. During training, the factor heads are anchored to the automatic layout, agent, visibility, and interaction targets of the training split, and a penalty on cross-channel covariance and kernel alignment limits shortcut mixing between channels. At inference the predictor reads only the encoder's context tokens: no detector, tracker, or agent proposal is needed, so the model runs wherever V-JEPA runs. Only the predictor and the top encoder blocks are trained. Unlike SlotFormer's object-centric slots \cite{wu2023slotformer} and DriveWorld's split of static scene from dynamics \cite{min2024driveworld}, FactorJEPA factorizes inside the JEPA embedding space and gives interactions their own supervised channel.

\section{Evaluation}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We adapt V-JEPA 2.1 at 2B and 1B parameters on a stratified 10k-clip set, and the 1B model also on the full corpus. FactorJEPA is compared with the frozen backbone, full fine-tuning, linear probing then fine-tuning, continual self-supervised learning, LoRA \cite{hu2022lora}, DoRA \cite{liu2024dora}, and Auto-RGN \cite{lee2023surgical}, all under identical clips, masking, optimization steps, and splits. Test clips are held out at the clip level (1,825 in the 10k regime, 23,106 in the full regime); clips cut from the same source video can fall on both sides. Every margin is a paired 95\% BCa bootstrap interval over test clips.

\paragraph{Diagnostics of predictive structure.}
\emph{Future-frame L1}: error between predicted and target-encoder embeddings on the token blocks hidden by V-JEPA's mask generator. \emph{Causal L1}: the same error when the second half of a clip is predicted from its first half. \emph{Mask-ratio slope}: growth of that error as the hidden fraction rises from 0.3 to 0.9. \emph{Motion cosine}: how well a linear probe recovers clip motion from the embedding. Nine further diagnostics probe temporal coherence and semantics (Table~\ref{tab:headline}).

\paragraph{Findings.}
With limited data (Table~\ref{tab:headline}), FactorJEPA separates from the best rival on Future-frame L1 and Causal L1 at both scales and on Mask-ratio slope at 1B, while Motion cosine favors generic fine-tuning. Full 115k-clip training reverses that deficit: FactorJEPA then separates on all four diagnostics (Figure~\ref{fig:forest}), by 13.9 (Causal L1) to 43.3 (Mask-ratio slope) interval widths, so the prediction versus motion trade-off is a data effect rather than a property of the factorization. Method rankings on the four diagnostics replicate from the 2B to the 1B backbone (Spearman $\rho \geq 0.895$). Below the rule, however, the best rival stays ahead at 115k on six diagnostics (rollout drift, exposure bias, L1-versus-$\Delta t$ decay, arrow-of-time, and the two TCC measures) by 8.3 to 146.5 interval widths. FactorJEPA predicts crowded scenes better one step ahead; it does not yet keep long predicted futures more coherent.

\section{What This Offers Mobility Agents}

For a vehicle or robot moving among pedestrians and mixed traffic, a world model that keeps agents and their interactions as separate channels is a natural place to read off who is likely to move next and who is coupled to whom, without a separate tracking stack at run time. Our results support that use only partly. One-step prediction and robustness to missing evidence, which matter when agents are occluded, improve consistently; temporal coherence over longer rollouts, which matters for planning several seconds ahead, does not. The model also needs only monocular video, with no HD map or LiDAR, which matters in the many cities where such maps do not exist and road users rarely follow lanes. DENSEWORLD-115k also gives a benchmark for this regime that is not tied to driving metrics, which we hope the workshop community will extend with interaction-level labels.

\section{Limitations}

Every diagnostic lives in the frozen V-JEPA embedding space or a linear probe on it; we report no downstream driving, planning, or forecasting task in pixel or trajectory space. We do not report the cross-channel leakage between layout, agent, and interaction channels that the factorization is meant to limit, and we do not isolate how much of the gain comes from the factor supervision rather than from retraining the predictor at all. The split is clip-level, so test clips can share a source video with training clips, and no test city is held out. All footage is Indian; other dense-traffic regions are untested. Each configuration was trained once.
