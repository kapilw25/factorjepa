# STEP 2: What each venue's reviewers will look for first

**Venue 1, Cog2Soc (bridge, due Sun Nov 29 AoE).** The call makes three items mandatory, so reviewers will check them on page 1 before anything else: (a) which human phenomenon the paper is about, stated in behavioural terms (for example how people move, attend or interact in crowds), (b) the research question or approach, and (c) the explicit link to a second participating community (cognitive science, sociology, HCI or computational social science). Calibrated on Group A, they reward a finding phrased about humans and a human-grounded label, not model scale; they will ask whether the "human behaviour" is measured or only inferred by automatic tools, how labels were calibrated or validated, what uncertainty is attached, whether the result replicates across cultures or sites, and whether responsible use of video of people is addressed. A paper whose contribution is a better video encoder will be read as off-topic unless the behavioural question leads.

**Venue 2, Data2AgentEx (bridge, due Tue Dec 1, 9:00 PM PST).** Reviewers will first look for the data-to-experience pipeline: what domain data was turned into what kind of experience, through which selection, curation or curriculum step, and how its lineage and quality were controlled. The theme "learning utility and transfer" names curricula, selection bias, training-test overlap and controlled evaluation of transfer, so they will check for a leak-free split, a matched control that isolates the data choice from the training recipe, and evidence that the curated experience transfers. Calibrated on Group B, a released dataset, benchmark or failure analysis carries weight; uncertainty in automatic labels (learned evaluators, evaluation shortcuts) and conditions for reuse of sensor data of people (consent, licences) will also be read early.

**Venue 3, Continual Causality (bridge, novel-work track, due Dec 1 AoE).** Reviewers will look first for the causal and the continual parts, named explicitly: what is non-stationary, what is the causal structure or mechanism that the model should keep or learn, and what shift, confounder or shortcut is at play. Calibrated on Group C, they reward a crisp concept (independent mechanisms, local spurious features, evolving causal knowledge) and a formal statement over large tables; continual pretraining of a foundation model is in scope, but only if the paper argues it in causal and continual-learning language (forgetting, plasticity, mechanism shift, shortcut). They will also check originality, since "works must be original", and how the concurrent student abstract and preprint relate.

**Venue 4, Human-Centric AgMS (workshop, due Fri Dec 25, 8:59 PM PST).** Reviewers will first ask where the human and the mobility service are: does the work help an agentic vehicle, fleet or traveller assistant perceive or predict road users, ground plans in perception, or build "world models of traffic scenes", and is it evaluated with "benchmarks beyond driving metrics"? Calibrated on Group D, they reward real road data, an interpretable metric tied to safety or behaviour of road users (pedestrians, two-wheelers, mixed traffic), and a deployment or accountability story. Early-stage work and negative results are welcome, so honesty about scope is safe, but the link to an agent that acts on roads must be concrete rather than implied.
