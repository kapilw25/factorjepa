# Evaluation of Draft 1: factorJEPA_v3_Cog2Soc.pdf (AAAI-27 Bridge: Cog2Soc)

## PART A. Acceptance probability

 * Acceptance probability at Cog2Soc: 62%
   * Reasoning: Cog2Soc explicitly asks papers to explain a human behavioural phenomenon, define an approach/research question, and connect AI to another participating field (transport research, computational social science, agent-based modeling), judging relevance, substance, and discussion value. The paper frames dense South Asian street negotiation clearly (Section 1, p. 1), builds a compelling cross-cultural dataset argument (Section 2, Figure 2, pp. 1–2), and links its three-stage curriculum directly to layered agent-based/social-force modeling (Section 5, p. 4). However, it loses points because every evaluation metric is an abstract self-supervised embedding diagnostic rather than a behavioural, trajectory, or human-validated outcome, and it concedes in Section 6 (p. 4) that it has not yet tested against any social-force/ABM model or checked interaction clips against human yielding judgments.
 * Acceptance probability at a typical AAAI-27 CV or Embodied-AI workshop: 78%
   * Reasoning: CV and embodied-AI workshops heavily reward strong backbone adaptation results (V-JEPA 2.1 at 1B and 2B scales), paired 95% BCa bootstrap intervals across 13 diagnostics, clean ablations separating staged updates from curated views (Table 2, p. 4), and a 276-hour reconstructible video dataset (Section 2, p. 1).
 * 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject:
   * Zero behavioural or human-validated metrics: All 13 diagnostics in Table 1 (p. 3) are embedding/linear-probe proxies; Section 6 (p. 4) explicitly states that trajectory forecasting, comparison with a social-force/ABM model, and human validation of interaction clips are "not reported here".
   * Clip-level train/test leakage across source videos and cities: Section 4 (p. 3) and Section 6 (p. 4) admit that clips from the same source video appear in both train and test splits and no city is held out, inflating spatial/background memorization.
   * Rivals beat FactorJEPA on 6 of 9 temporal coherence metrics: Table 1 (p. 3) and Figure 3 (p. 3) show LoRA/frozen models outperforming FactorJEPA by 8.3 to 146.5 interval widths on rollout drift, exposure bias, arrow-of-time, and TCC—undermining its utility for multi-step behavioural simulation (acknowledged in Section 5, p. 4).
 * 3 strongest points:
   * Crisp cross-cultural behavioural framing and empirical density proof: Figure 2 (p. 2) quantifies higher agent counts and pixel occupancy across six scene types against BDD100K/nuScenes, grounding the cultural/regional modeling gap.
   * Rigorous paired statistical protocol and decomposition ablation: Table 1 (p. 3) compares seven rivals across 1B/2B and 10k/115k regimes with 95% BCa bootstrap intervals, while Table 2 (p. 4) cleanly isolates the staged schedule (RAW) from the semantic clips (Ours).
   * Thoughtful copyright and privacy governance: Section 2 ("Release and responsible use", p. 2) ships reconstructible YouTube metadata under CC BY 4.0 with creator opt-out and explicitly restricts use to aggregate street behaviour rather than individual tracking.

 * E.3) Rewritten Abstract (Cog2Soc):
   > We introduce DENSEWORLD-115k, a 276-hour corpus of 115,687 clips from 714 public YouTube videos across 22 Indian cities, and FactorJEPA, a three-stage curriculum that adapts the video world model V-JEPA 2.1 to dense, non-lane-structured street behaviour. On crowded South Asian streets, pedestrians, two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, carts, and animals share one right of way and coordinate through local negotiation while frequently occluded, a regime rarely captured in Western driving datasets. Mirroring layered behavioural models, FactorJEPA adapts V-JEPA 2.1 sequentially on clips of street layout, individual road users, and their interactions. Across seven adaptation baselines under identical clips and budgets, a single fixed FactorJEPA configuration improves masked and future embedding prediction at both 2B and 1B scales; at 115k clips, it outperforms the strongest rival on all four diagnostics of predictive structure by 13.9 to 43.3 widths of the paired 95% confidence interval. Ablations on raw clips show that staged adaptation drives most of the accuracy gain, while structured layout, agent, and interaction views flatten degradation under heavy occlusion; competing methods still lead on multi-step temporal coherence. Together, DENSEWORLD-115k and FactorJEPA provide an empirical foundation for cross-cultural modelling of collective street behaviour.
