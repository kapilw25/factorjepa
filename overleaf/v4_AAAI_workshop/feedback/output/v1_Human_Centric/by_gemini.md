# Evaluation of Draft 4: factorJEPA_v1_Human_Centric.pdf (AAAI-27 Workshop W4: Human-Centric AgMS)

## PART A. Acceptance probability

 * Acceptance probability at Human-Centric AgMS: 88%
   * Reasoning: Human-Centric AgMS explicitly targets "agentic AI for vehicles, fleets and traveller assistants," including Theme (A) "grounding plans in perception and 'world models of traffic scenes'" and Theme (D) "trustworthy deployment on real roads, including 'benchmarks beyond driving metrics'"—and explicitly welcomes early-stage work, benchmark proposals, and negative results. Draft 4 hits every single button: its title directly echoes Theme A ("Can a World Model of Traffic Scenes Predict Crowded South Asian Streets?", p. 1), Section 5 connects monocular-only inference to mapless mixed-traffic deployment and explicitly offers DENSEWORLD-115k as a benchmark "not tied to driving metrics" (p. 4), and its candid reporting of rollout-coherence losses fits the workshop's welcome for negative results. Furthermore, the 4-week window before Dec 25 gives ample time to resolve its minor gaps.
 * Acceptance probability at a typical AAAI-27 CV or Embodied-AI workshop: 78%
   * Reasoning: Solid backbone scaling, 13-diagnostic statistical evaluation, and 276-hour dataset release, limited mainly by the lack of a downstream task head.
 * 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject:
   * No downstream mobility prediction or planning evaluation yet: Section 6 (p. 4) notes that trajectory forecasting and human yielding validation are not reported yet, and rivals lead on multi-step rollout coherence needed for planning several seconds ahead (Section 5, p. 4).
   * Unverified quality of automatic road-user and interaction labels: The detector/tracker/proximity pipeline has no human precision/recall check yet (Section 6, p. 4), which matters for trustworthy real-road benchmarks.
   * Clip-level train/test split and lack of vulnerable-road-user (VRU) breakdown: Source videos overlap between train and test (Section 4, p. 3), and while the abstract highlights pedestrians, two-wheelers, rickshaws, carts, and animals (p. 1), Section 4 does not disaggregate performance on vulnerable road users.
 * 3 strongest points:
   * Exact alignment with Workshop Themes A and D: Directly addresses world models of unstructured, mapless traffic scenes without relying on HD maps, LiDAR, or Western lane-based driving metrics (Sections 1, 2, 5, pp. 1–2, 4).
   * Strong empirical & statistical evidence across 2B/1B scales and 7 baselines: Table 1 (p. 3), Figure 3 (p. 3), and Table 2 (p. 4) establish clear, statistically separated improvements in single-step prediction and occlusion robustness (mask-ratio slope) with zero extra inference cost.
   * Ethical, copyright-safe, and inclusive data design: Figure 2 (p. 2) proves dramatic density gaps in Western datasets, while Section 2 (p. 2) establishes responsible metadata-only release for aggregate street behaviour.

 * E.3) Rewritten Abstract (Human-Centric AgMS):
   > We introduce DENSEWORLD-115k, a 276-hour corpus of 115,687 clips from 714 public YouTube videos across 22 Indian cities, and FactorJEPA, a three-stage curriculum that adapts the video world model V-JEPA 2.1 to dense, non-lane-structured traffic. Mobility agents operating in crowded cities must anticipate pedestrians, two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, carts, and animals that coordinate through negotiation while frequently occluded, a regime rarely captured in Western driving benchmarks. FactorJEPA adapts V-JEPA 2.1 sequentially on clips of street layout, individual road users, and their interactions, requiring only monocular video at inference. Across seven adaptation baselines under identical clips and budgets, one fixed configuration improves masked and future embedding prediction at both 2B and 1B scales; at 115k clips, it outperforms the strongest rival on all four diagnostics of predictive structure by 13.9 to 43.3 widths of the paired 95% confidence interval. Ablations on raw clips show that staged adaptation carries most of the accuracy gain, while structured views flatten degradation under heavy occlusion; competing methods still lead on temporal coherence.
