# Evaluation of Draft 3: factorJEPA_v5_Continual_Causality.pdf (AAAI-27 Bridge: Continual Causality, Novel-Work Track)

## PART A. Acceptance probability

 * Acceptance probability at Continual Causality Bridge: 54%
   * Reasoning: The Continual Causality Bridge focuses on "how learning systems can continually build and maintain accurate causal world models when the world is non-stationary and only partially observed," and its novel-work track requires original research. Draft 3 smartly frames adapting V-JEPA 2.1 from Western/general video to dense South Asian streets as continual pretraining under distribution shift and partial observability (Section 1, p. 1), includes continual self-supervised learning among its 7 rivals (Section 4, p. 3), and directly connects mask-ratio slope to partial observability (Section 5, p. 4). However, it faces a steep uphill battle with causality-focused reviewers because Section 5 (p. 4) explicitly disavows any causal claim ("We make no causal claim: Causal L1 names the direction of prediction... not an identified cause") and concedes that backward retention on the original distribution has not yet been tested ("Whether the adapted model keeps its accuracy on the original distribution is the next test", p. 4)—missing the two pillars of Continual and Causality.
 * Acceptance probability at a typical AAAI-27 CV or Embodied-AI workshop: 78%
   * Reasoning: Standard CV/embodied workshops evaluate representation quality, ablations, and dataset contribution without requiring formal causal identification or backward-transfer retention curves.
 * 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject:
   * No backward-transfer / forgetting evaluation: The title and Section 1 frame the work as continual pretraining/adaptation, yet Section 5 (p. 4) admits that retention on the original pretraining/Western distribution has not been measured—a core requirement in continual learning.
   * Explicit absence of causal modeling or causal-structure evaluation: Section 5 (p. 4) states "We make no causal claim," and there is no intervention/counterfactual test or causal-graph discovery, making the "Causality" half of the bridge feel purely metaphorical.
   * Severe degradation on long-horizon rollout diagnostics: On a bridge dedicated to world models, losing to LoRA/frozen baselines by 8.3 to 146.5 interval widths on rollout drift, exposure bias, arrow-of-time, and TCC (Table 1 & Figure 3, p. 3) suggests that staged adaptation harms multi-step world-model dynamics.
 * 3 strongest points:
   * Compelling real-world domain-shift & partial-observability framing: Framing dense Indian street video as an extreme distribution shift with heavy occlusion (Figure 2, p. 2) offers a much richer testbed than synthetic arithmetic/colored-shape benchmarks typical of continual-causality workshops.
   * Direct evidence that update schedule and scene factorization matter under shift: Table 2 (p. 4) proves that staged surgical unfreezing + frozen teacher + selective projection decay (RAW) beats plain continued pretraining and LoRA on forward prediction, while factorized views (Ours) specifically improve robustness to partial observability (mask-ratio slope).
   * Clean empirical rigor across scales and 7 baselines: Including continual self-supervised learning, surgical fine-tuning (Auto-RGN), selective projection decay, LoRA, and DoRA under identical budgets with 95% BCa intervals (Section 4, p. 3) meets high empirical standards.

   > Adapting a pretrained video world model to a new traffic culture poses a sharp continual-learning challenge under distribution shift and partial observability: scenes become denser, road-user types diversify, and actors are frequently occluded. We study this shift by adapting V-JEPA 2.1 to DENSEWORLD-115k, a corpus of 115,687 clips (276 hours) from 714 public YouTube videos across 22 Indian cities, released as reconstructible metadata. FactorJEPA adapts the model in three stages aligned with scene structure—street layout, individual road users, and their interactions—using progressive encoder unfreezing, a frozen teacher, and selective projection decay. Compared with seven adaptation baselines, including continual self-supervised learning, under identical clips and compute budgets, one fixed configuration improves masked and future embedding prediction at both 2B and 1B scales; at 115k clips, it outperforms the strongest rival on all four diagnostics of predictive structure by 13.9 to 43.3 widths of the paired 95% confidence interval. Ablations on raw clips show that the staged schedule drives most of the accuracy gain, while structured clips slow degradation as more of the scene is hidden; competing methods still lead on temporal coherence.
