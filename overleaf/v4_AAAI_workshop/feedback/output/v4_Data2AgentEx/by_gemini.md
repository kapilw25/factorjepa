# Evaluation of Draft 2: factorJEPA_v4_Data2AgentEx.pdf (AAAI-27 Bridge: Data2AgentEx)

## PART A. Acceptance probability

 * Acceptance probability at Data2AgentEx: 84%
   * Reasoning: Data2AgentEx asks "how can domain knowledge, data, and workflows become grounded, informative, and responsibly governed experience for learning and evaluating agents?" and explicitly welcomes datasets, benchmarks, and failure analyses across its five themes. Draft 2 is an almost exact Bullseye for this CFP: Section 2 covers experience production, lineage, and governance (reconstructible YouTube metadata, CC BY 4.0, creator takedowns, consent boundaries, pp. 1–2); Section 3 operationalizes workflow representation without human labels (Grounding DINO + SAM 3 + relative-motion rule → layout/agent/interaction views, p. 2); and Section 4–5 directly answer learning utility and transfer by separating the schedule from the curated views via a controlled ablation ladder (Table 2, p. 4) and reporting wins and losses honestly.
 * Acceptance probability at a typical AAAI-27 CV or Embodied-AI workshop: 78%
   * Reasoning: Same technical backbone, ablations, and dataset scale, though CV reviewers might ask for one downstream task head (e.g., trajectory or box prediction) alongside embedding diagnostics.
 * 3 things most likely to make a reviewer reject:
   * Unverified quality of the automatic curation pipeline: Section 5 (p. 4) admits that the detector, tracker, and proximity rule have no human quality check yet ("a human check of missed and false road users and of who yields to whom is the next step"), which directly touches Data2AgentEx's Verification and evaluation theme.
   * Clip-level train/test overlap across source videos: Section 4 (p. 3) and Section 6 (p. 4) note that clips from the same source video appear in both train and test, which Data2AgentEx explicitly flags under Learning utility and transfer ("training-test overlap, and controlled evaluation of transfer").
   * No downstream agent policy or closed-loop rollouts: All 13 diagnostics are open-loop embedding/linear-probe metrics (Table 1, p. 3), and rivals lead on multi-step rollout coherence (rollout drift, exposure bias, TCC), leaving open whether this curated experience actually improves a downstream embodied/navigation agent.
 * 3 strongest points:
   * Direct, point-by-point alignment with the bridge's data-centric questions: Section 5 (p. 4) systematically maps empirical evidence onto learning utility (Table 2 ladder), label validity, training-test overlap (source-video bootstrap resampling across 127 videos), and conditions for reuse.
   * Clean decomposition of schedule vs. curated views (Table 2, p. 4): Proving that staged updates drive L1 gains while automatic semantic views specifically buy occlusion robustness (mask-ratio slope) is an exemplary data-centric finding.
   * Reconstructible, legally and ethically governed dataset release (Section 2, p. 2): Shipping clip manifests + deterministic YouTube rebuild scripts under CC BY 4.0 solves copyright/storage bottlenecks while documenting creator removal and non-consent constraints.

   > We present a label-free data curation pipeline and benchmark that convert raw public street video into staged training experience for video world models. DENSEWORLD-115k comprises 115,687 clips (276 hours) from 714 public YouTube videos across 22 Indian cities, released as reconstructible metadata. For 86,831 clips, an automatic detector, tracker, and relative-motion rule decompose each scene into three structured views: street layout with road users blurred, road users with background suppressed, and crops around interacting pairs. FactorJEPA adapts V-JEPA 2.1 on these views via a three-stage curriculum. Across seven adaptation baselines under identical clips and compute budgets, a single fixed configuration improves masked and future embedding prediction at both 2B and 1B scales; at 115k clips, it outperforms the strongest rival on all four diagnostics of predictive structure by 13.9 to 43.3 widths of the paired 95% confidence interval.
